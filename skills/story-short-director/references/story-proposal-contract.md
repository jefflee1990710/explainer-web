# Story Proposal Contract (Phase A)

Present a readable director's proposal and stop for confirmation before writing model prompts.

## Adapt the source into a story

- Follow the source first. If it already has events, speakers, and an order, keep them. Do not rewrite it into want → obstacle → turn → resolution unless that is already how the source moves.
- Extract the single point or feeling the source leaves. Everything on screen should serve that — without inventing a lecture.
- Choose a protagonist the audience recognises (a person like the viewer, or the cast provided).
- Cut only what the duration cannot hold. Do not cut a beat just because it is not "want" or "turn".

Spoken words scale with the preset (~2.2–2.5 words/second, minus any silent beat):

- **4–8s micro**: ~10–15 words, 1–2 clips.
- **15–20s short**: ~30–45 words, 2–4 clips.
- **30–45s punchy**: ~60–100 words, 4–6 clips.
- **50–60s full**: ~110–140 words, 7–10 clips.

## Header contract

1. Title in the reference language and in English
2. Total duration, clip count N (each 3–8s), loop mode: always Linear (the story ends)
3. Core message and how Clip 1 opens (from the source, not a stock hook)
4. Aspect ratio and the one or two locations with anchor props
5. No narrator — characters speak; spoken-unit count is dialogue only
6. Cast lock: follow attached blueprints exactly, or the single defined protagonist
7. The clip-by-clip arc in the source's order (not a canned six-beat map)

## Storyboard contract

Produce N rows of 3–8 seconds:

| Clip # & time | Story beat | Scene at clip start | Motion, camera & handoff | Dialogue (no narrator) | BGM / SFX |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules per row:

- `Story beat` names this clip's job in the source's language (a scene, a line of dialogue, a change). Do not require the labels hook / want / obstacle / turn / resolution / button.
- `Scene at clip start` describes exactly what is visible at t=0: protagonist pose, expression, props, location. It is the start keyframe.
- `Motion, camera & handoff` describes the modest continuation to the end keyframe (same shot), the camera move, and what the next clip inherits.
- Emotion first: name the character's feeling; choose gesture and framing to show it.
- Something meaningful changes every 1.5–2.5 seconds: gesture, prop, camera, expression.

## Composition by aspect ratio

- `16:9`: lateral staging; travel and opposing characters can sit left-to-right.
- `9:16`: vertical staging; foreground/background depth; keep faces in the upper-middle third.
- `1:1`: centre-weighted; short travel; important objects enter from the edge.

## Palette and text

- Colours in ordinary words only. One accent colour is fine when the source has a motif object.
- No captions or subtitles. In-world text only when it is a story prop (a sign, a note), kept to one to three words and spelled exactly.

## Confirmation ending

Ask the user to approve the proposal, revise a named beat or line, or change a global setting (ratio, cast, voice, pacing).

## Phase A checks

- The clip order matches the source story (or the smallest invented story if the source had no plot).
- No forced want / obstacle / turn / resolution template.
- Cast lock statement present; no invented restyling of attached blueprints.
- Every clip 3–8s; start and end are the same shot; the next row inherits the previous end.
- The last row rests and does not loop.
- No technical colour notation, no captions, no invented facts.
