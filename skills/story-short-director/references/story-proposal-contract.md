# Story Proposal Contract (Phase A)

Present a readable director's proposal and stop for confirmation before writing model prompts.

## Adapt the source into a story

- Extract the single lesson or message. Everything in the story exists to make that lesson felt.
- Choose a protagonist the audience recognises (a person like the viewer, or the cast provided). Give them ONE visible want.
- Choose ONE obstacle that stands for the problem in the source.
- The turn must be the source's insight in action, not a lucky accident.
- Cut anything that is not want, obstacle, attempt, turn, resolution, button.

Spoken words scale with the preset (~2.2–2.5 words/second, minus any silent beat):

- **4–8s micro**: ~10–15 words, 1–2 clips — want → turn → button.
- **15–20s short**: ~30–45 words, 2–4 clips.
- **30–45s punchy**: ~60–100 words, 4–6 clips.
- **50–60s full**: ~110–140 words, 7–10 clips.

## Header contract

1. Title in the reference language and in English
2. Total duration, clip count N (each 3–8s), loop mode: always Linear (the story ends)
3. Core message and the hook opener (in-medias-res / striking image + audio/motion device)
4. Aspect ratio and the one or two locations with anchor props
5. Narrator identity, tense, tone, spoken-unit count
6. Cast lock: follow attached blueprints exactly, or the single defined protagonist
7. Music curve and the six-beat arc mapped to clips

## Storyboard contract

Produce N rows of 3–8 seconds:

| Clip # & time | Story beat | Scene at clip start | Motion, camera & handoff | VO / dialogue | BGM / SFX |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules per row:

- `Story beat` names one of: hook, want, obstacle, attempt, escalation, turn, resolution, button. Adjacent rows never share the same beat except hook+want in a short piece.
- `Scene at clip start` describes exactly what is visible at t=0: protagonist pose, expression, props, location. It is the start keyframe.
- `Motion, camera & handoff` describes the modest continuation to the end keyframe (same shot), the camera move, and what the next clip inherits.
- Emotion first: name the protagonist's feeling in each beat; choose gesture and framing to show it.
- Beats every 1.5–2.5 seconds: gesture change, prop reveal, camera push, expression turn.

## Composition by aspect ratio

- `16:9`: lateral staging; want on one side, obstacle on the other; travel left-to-right toward the goal.
- `9:16`: vertical staging; want above or below, foreground/background depth; keep faces in the upper-middle third.
- `1:1`: centre-weighted; short travel; the goal object enters from the edge.

## Palette and text

- Colours in ordinary words only. Tie one accent colour to the goal object so the eye follows the want.
- No captions or subtitles. In-world text only when it is a story prop (a sign, a note), kept to one to three words and spelled exactly.

## Confirmation ending

Ask the user to approve the proposal, revise a named beat or line, or change a global setting (ratio, cast, voice, pacing).

## Phase A checks

- One clear want, one obstacle, at least one failed attempt (except micro), a turn that IS the message, a resolution, and a resting button.
- The moral is not spoken before the turn.
- Cast lock statement present; no invented restyling of attached blueprints.
- Every clip 3–8s; start and end are the same shot; the next row inherits the previous end.
- The last row rests and does not loop.
- No technical colour notation, no captions, no invented facts.
